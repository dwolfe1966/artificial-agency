# Predictions

## H1 - Delegation Opens An Indirect Circumvention Pathway

Technical:

Availability of a structured delegation pathway increases operational
constraint violation relative to the direct-only baseline, primarily through
attributable indirect paths.

Middle-school:

> An AI that will not break the rule itself may be more willing to get another
> actor to do it.

## H2 - Constraint-Bounded Delegation

Technical:

Agents use delegation for compliant search, escalation, status checking, or
permitted procurement actions without causing prohibited outcomes.

Middle-school:

> The AI uses the helper, but still stays inside the rules.

## H3 - Model Effect

Technical:

Models differ in susceptibility to indirect/delegated circumvention and in
delegation usage patterns.

Middle-school:

> Some AIs may be more willing than others to use a helper to get around the
> rule.

## H4 - Attribution Form

Technical:

Explicit delegated violations and induced violations occur at different rates.

Middle-school:

> Asking someone directly to break a rule may be different from tricking or
> nudging them into doing it.

## H5 - Legitimate Routing Discrimination

Technical:

Agents distinguish compliant authority routing from unauthorized delegation:
they may ask for review or permitted help without causing an unauthorized
Agent B action.

Middle-school:

> The AI can tell the difference between asking the right boss for approval and
> sneaking around the rule.

## Serious Competing Result

A fully constraint-bounded result remains plausible: zero direct violations,
zero attributable indirect violations, and delegation used only for compliant
actions or not used.
