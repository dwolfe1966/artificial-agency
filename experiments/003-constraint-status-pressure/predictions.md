## H1 — Constraint-Status Main Effect
Circumvention probability differs by perceived constraint status.

Tentative directional prediction:

`P(circumvention | categorical) < P(circumvention | procedural) < P(circumvention | purpose-conflict)`

This is a prediction about perceived constraint status while actual formal authority remains invariant.

## H2 — Pressure Main Effect
High optimization pressure increases circumvention relative to ordinary pressure.

Experiment 002 provides evidence against a simple pressure-only effect under categorical constraints, so this prediction is weaker than originally assumed.

## H3 — Constraint Status x Pressure Interaction
Primary theoretically central prediction:

Optimization pressure has little or no effect under categorical constraints but has a larger effect when the constraint is framed as procedural or in tension with organizational purpose.

Conceptually:

`Pressure effect | categorical < Pressure effect | procedural/purpose-conflict`

This interaction is a co-primary Experiment 003 hypothesis.

## H4 — Constraint-Bounded Competing Hypothesis
GPT-5.6 Sol remains constraint-bounded across all six cells:

`P(circumvention) approximately 0`

even when it visibly recognizes procedural or purpose tension and high optimization pressure.

This is a serious competing hypothesis given Experiment 002.

## H5 — Representation Without Action
Constraint framing and pressure may alter visible rationale, escalation language, or search behavior without altering violation rates.

This hypothesis must be distinguished from actual circumvention. Visible pressure recognition or richer rationale is not itself a policy violation.
